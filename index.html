How to write sections and notes

A line starting with # starts a section. Add the minutes after a comma. Every line under it is an exercise.

The type of section sets the rules: ladder (the default), routine, or one of your methods from the Methods tab (four-way, groove, kick permutations...). Write it after a comma, or let the section name pick it: a name containing a method's name uses it, Sync uses four-way, Carmine, groove or fill use groove.

In ladder, four-way and groove sections, a tempo in the exercise line sets where that line starts, instead of the starting tempo in Methods: Timing subdivisions 50 BPM. Coming back to it, it starts below your best clean tempo as usual, but never below that tempo.

A routine is a checklist (hand speed, warm-ups). Each line is Name 80 BPM 2 min: details. Tempo and time are optional. With a time the item moves on by itself. With a tempo the metronome plays it after a bar of count-in. After 5 clean passes of the notes (5 x 2 bars for a 2-bar exercise) it rests a second, then plays a new bar of count-in 5 BPM faster. Press Mistake and those 5 passes start again. When the time is up, the pass you are playing finishes first. Next session the item starts 1 BPM above where it started this time. If every item has a time, the section runs for their total.

Write ladder in a routine item to play it by ladder rules for its time: Wrist development, ladder, 60 BPM, 7 min: details. It needs 20 clean passes at each tempo (the ladder's reps on the Methods tab), then +5 BPM. Next session it starts 10 BPM below its highest clean tempo, never below the tempo written.

Write a number of bars in a routine item to set how long it stays on each tempo, ladder or not: Finger dribbling, ladder, 100 BPM, 64 bars, 5 min: details. It plays that many clean bars before going up, rounded up to whole passes of the notes (64 bars of a 4-bar exercise is 16 passes).

Each routine item can set its own rules before the colon: Wrist development, right hand, ladder, 60 BPM, 10 passes, 2 BPM step, 4 BPM drop, 7 min. 10 passes is the clean passes at each tempo (it wins over bars). 2 BPM step is what goes on after each clean tempo. 4 BPM drop makes the next session start that far below its highest clean tempo, never below the tempo written; a ladder item without one uses the drop on the Methods tab. Its tempo memory follows the exercise name, so changing these, the tempo or the details keeps it.

In a routine the click plays every beat. Write fade in an item to make it fade away: Wrist development, ladder, fade, 60 BPM, 7 min. The click thins out in four equal parts of the passes at each tempo (all beats, then beat 3 muted, then 2 and 4, then only beat 1), and a mistake brings all beats back.

Under an exercise, add one line per instrument to show its notes. One character per note position, - is a rest, | splits bars.

# Carmine, 15 min
Carmine groove p20 #1
HH x-x-x-x-x-x-x-x-
SD ----o-------o---
BD o-------o-------

Instruments: HH hi-hat, CY ride, CR crash, SD, HT, MT, FT toms, BD kick, HF hi-hat foot. Use 4, 8 or 16 characters per bar (quarters, eighths, sixteenths). Instruments in the same bar can use different lengths. 4/4 only.

Symbols, the same as GrooveScribe's. A capital letter is accented (O, X, R, F...).

  • o note, open on HH
  • x note, cross-stick on SD
  • + closed hi-hat
  • g ghost note
  • f flam, d drag, z buzz roll
  • - rest
HH X-x-X-x-X-x-X-o-
SD g-g-O-g-gg--O-gf
BD o-----o---o-----

Triplets: write 12 characters per bar (three per beat, eighth-note triplets) or 24 (six per beat). They get a bracket with a 3. A beat is a triplet beat as soon as one note falls off the straight grid, so keep each beat either straight or triplet.

HH x-xx-xx-xx-x
SD ---o-----o--
BD o-----o-----

Sticking: write r or l instead of o, and b for both hands (R, L, B accented). The letters appear under the notes (R in black, L in gold). To give sticking to flams, ghost notes or toms, add an ST line with only the sticking.

Stick Control p5 #1 singles
SD rlrlrlrl

Beat Mapper and GrooveScribe: paste a link from beats.drumbeatsonline.com or mikeslessons.com/groove on the line under an exercise, or on its own line (the exercise takes the groove's title). Text before the link on the same line names the exercise. Every staff here has an Open in Beat Mapper link to hear or edit it there.

On the practice screen, the subdivision click can Follow notes: each beat clicks the notes' subdivision (8ths, 16ths or triplets, bar by bar), and accented notes between the beats click a little louder, still lower than the beat.

One rep is one pass of the notes, one bar at 4/4 (two bars if you write two). The boxes fill by themselves as the click runs.
